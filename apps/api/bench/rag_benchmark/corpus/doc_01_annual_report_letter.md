# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Annual Report 2025: Letter to Shareholders

*Alasdair Pennhallow, Chair, and Ingrid Vasterholm, Chief Executive Officer*

Dear shareholders,

Fiscal 2025 asked a great deal of this company, and our people answered. We made difficult decisions about parts of the business that were no longer earning their place, we absorbed an unplanned interruption at one of our busiest plants, and we still finished the year with a stronger balance sheet than the one we started it with. This letter sets out how we think about the year and what we intend to do next.

### A year of unevenness

The three businesses that make up Tarnwick did not move in step. Demand for our flow-control products stayed firm, helped by long-cycle infrastructure spending and a steady replacement market. Our field services business kept winning multi-year maintenance contracts, particularly with operators of water and power assets. Precision components, our oldest business, had the roughest twelve months: customers destocked early in the year, and operational trouble later on made the recovery harder than it needed to be.

The detail is covered elsewhere in this report, so we will not repeat it here. What we want to stress is that the underlying franchise remains intact. Order books at year end were healthier than at the same point a year ago, and our customers' qualification programmes with us are, if anything, deeper than before.

### Our people

Tarnwick closed the year with 11,462 full-time colleagues across 23 sites in 14 countries, counted as of the last day of September. That is slightly higher than a year earlier, even after the decisions we took on our footprint, because hiring in field services and in engineering more than offset reductions elsewhere. We are proud of how colleagues affected by site changes were treated, and of the redeployment and retraining support that was offered to them.

Safety remains the measure we look at first. Our lost-time injury rate fell to 0.41 per 200,000 hours worked, the best figure in the company's history. Two sites completed the year without a single recordable incident. We will not treat that as a finish line; one serious injury is one too many, and the Board reviews every such event personally.

### Customers and technology

On-time delivery to customers averaged 96.2% for the year, up from the previous year, despite the disruption we have mentioned. Customers have told us that what they value is not only the product but the predictability around it: accurate lead times, honest communication when something slips, and engineers who pick up the phone. We filed 37 new patent applications during the year, the majority in sensing and in materials for high-pressure sealing.

We also continued to invest in digital tools for our field engineers. Technicians in our services business now carry a diagnostic application that links directly to asset histories, and early results show fewer repeat visits per customer site. It is not glamorous work, but it is the kind of improvement that compounds.

### Capital allocation

Our priorities have not changed. First, invest in the franchise: the plants, the tooling and the people who make our products. Second, keep the balance sheet conservative enough that we can act when opportunities appear and ride out the periods when they do not. Third, return surplus cash to shareholders through a dividend that we aim to grow steadily and, where appropriate, through repurchases. We believe a disciplined approach to acquisitions belongs in the first of those categories rather than being a separate ambition; we would rather do a few things we understand deeply than many things we understand superficially.

### Governance and the Board

We were pleased to welcome Thaddeus Okonjo-Reyes to the Board in March. His experience in industrial automation has already been valuable in our discussions on factory investment. The Board met nine times during the year, including two sessions held at operating sites, which we find far more informative than any slide deck. Committee reports follow later in this document.

### Looking ahead

We enter fiscal 2026 with a clear agenda: restore the Precision Components business to its historical performance, keep growing in flow control and services, and keep our costs and our promises under control. We are grateful to our colleagues, customers, suppliers and shareholders for their continued support.

Alasdair Pennhallow
Chair

Ingrid Vasterholm
