# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Notes to the Consolidated Income Statement, Fiscal Year 2025

*Extract. Amounts are in millions of US dollars unless stated otherwise.*

### Note 3: Operating costs

Research and development expense was $112.4 million (fiscal 2024: $104.9 million), reflecting continued investment in sensing technology and sealing materials. Selling and administrative expenses rose modestly, driven by wage inflation and higher variable compensation in the services business, partly offset by lower professional fees. Depreciation and amortisation were broadly flat compared with the prior year.

### Note 4: Restructuring

During the second half of the year the Company completed the closure of the Halbrook foundry, a casting operation that had been in service since 1961. The foundry's output has been absorbed by other facilities in the near term and a replacement is planned separately. Charges of $48.6 million were recognised in the year in connection with the closure, comprising severance and related benefits, accelerated depreciation of equipment that was taken out of service early, and site decommissioning costs.

The programme affected 640 roles. The large majority of the employees concerned had left the business by the balance sheet date, and the remainder will leave during the first half of fiscal 2026. Remaining cash payments are not expected to be material to liquidity. The Company does not currently anticipate further restructuring programmes of comparable size.

### Note 5: Goodwill impairment

The Company tests goodwill for impairment annually in the fourth quarter, and more often if events indicate a possible loss of value. In the fourth quarter a reduced forecast for municipal water-meter order volumes caused the carrying amount of the Aquaflow Controls reporting unit to exceed its recoverable amount. A non-cash goodwill impairment of $91.2 million was therefore recognised.

The recoverable amount was determined on a value-in-use basis, using five-year cash flow projections approved by management. Key assumptions were a pre-tax discount rate of 9.8% and a long-term growth rate of 2.0%. A further one-percentage-point increase in the discount rate would give rise to additional impairment on the unit. Goodwill allocated to the Company's other reporting units was tested and no impairment was identified; headroom in each case exceeded 20%.

### Note 6: Income taxes

The effective tax rate for the year was 23.1% (fiscal 2024: 22.6%). The goodwill impairment is largely non-deductible for tax purposes, which raised the effective rate relative to the statutory blend. The benefit of research credits and favourable resolution of two prior-year audits partly offset this. Deferred tax assets of the Company's German and Polish subsidiaries were reviewed and no valuation allowance was considered necessary.

### Note 7: Net finance costs

Net finance costs for the year were $71.8 million. The charge includes $3.2 million for the write-off of unamortised arrangement fees on the early repayment of a term facility, which is presented within interest expense. Interest income on cash balances rose with higher average deposits during the second half. Foreign exchange gains and losses on financing balances were immaterial.

### Note 8: Earnings per share

Basic and diluted earnings per share are presented on the face of the income statement. The weighted average number of shares used in the calculation decreased slightly over the year following the completion of open-market repurchases early in the period. There were no anti-dilutive instruments excluded from the diluted calculation.
