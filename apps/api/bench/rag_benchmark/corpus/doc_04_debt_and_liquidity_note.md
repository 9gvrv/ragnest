# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Note 12: Borrowings and Liquidity (Fiscal Year 2025)

*Amounts are in millions of US dollars unless stated otherwise.*

### Overview

At 30 September 2025 the Company's gross borrowings consisted of two series of fixed-rate senior notes. The balance sheet also shows lease liabilities, which are described in Note 14 and are not included here. During the year the Company materially changed the shape of its debt: it issued new long-dated notes, replaced its revolving credit agreement, and retired a term facility that had been in place since 2022.

| Instrument | Principal / commitment | Drawn at 30 Sep 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Senior notes due 2028 | $350 million | $350 million |
| Senior notes due 2031 | $600 million | $600 million |
| Revolving credit facility | $750 million | None |

Cash and cash equivalents at year end were $412.7 million. Net leverage, measured as net debt to adjusted EBITDA under the definitions in the facility agreement, was 2.1 times.

### Senior notes

The 2028 notes bear interest at 4.90% per year, payable semi-annually, and were issued in a prior period. In March 2025 the Company priced $600 million of new senior notes maturing in 2031. They pay a fixed coupon of 5.375% per year, also semi-annually, and rank equally with the Company's other senior unsecured obligations. Demand for the offering was roughly three times the amount sold, and the issue was rated one notch above the Company's long-standing rating floor by one of the two agencies that cover it. Both series of notes contain customary covenants limiting liens and certain sale-and-leaseback transactions, but neither contains financial maintenance covenants.

### Use of proceeds and repayment of the term loan

The net proceeds of the 2031 notes were applied to repay in full the $380 million term loan provided by Brandt-Lyle Bank. That loan had been drawn in 2022 to finance the purchase of the Aquaflow Controls business and was scheduled to amortise over several years. Repayment eliminated the Company's only floating-rate term debt and extended the average maturity of its borrowings by roughly two and a half years. The write-off of arrangement fees on the early repayment is described in Note 7.

### Revolving credit facility

In March 2025 the Company entered into a new $750 million revolving credit facility with a syndicate of eleven banks, replacing the previous agreement. The facility matures on 14 March 2030, with two one-year extension options exercisable at the lenders' discretion. Borrowings bear interest at a benchmark rate plus a margin that steps up or down with the Company's leverage. The Company pays a commitment fee on undrawn amounts.

The facility requires the Company to maintain a net leverage ratio of no more than 3.25 times, tested at each half-year end. The Company was in compliance with the covenant throughout the year, with substantial headroom. No amounts were drawn at 30 September 2025 or at any point during the second half of the fiscal year, and the facility remained fully available for general corporate purposes. Letters of credit issued under a separate bilateral line totalled less than $20 million.

### Maturity profile

The Company has no scheduled debt maturities in fiscal 2026 or fiscal 2027. The 2028 notes are the next principal repayment, followed by the 2031 notes. Management intends to address the 2028 maturity well in advance and will consider the level of cash generation, the pipeline of investment opportunities and market conditions when it does.

### Credit ratings

The Company is rated investment grade by two independent agencies. Ratings were affirmed during the year with a stable outlook. Management believes that conservative leverage is a strategic asset: it protects the dividend, supports customers' confidence in long-term supply contracts, and preserves flexibility for acquisitions that meet the Company's return thresholds.
