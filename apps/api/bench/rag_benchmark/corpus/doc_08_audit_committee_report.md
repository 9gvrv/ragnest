# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Report of the Audit Committee, Fiscal Year 2025

*Priyanka Delacroix-Oyelaran, Chair of the Audit Committee*

### Introduction

On behalf of the Board, I am pleased to present the Audit Committee's report for the year ended 30 September 2025. The Committee is made up entirely of independent non-executive directors, all of whom have recent and relevant financial experience. It met seven times during the year, and the external auditor attended each meeting, including private sessions without management.

### Our role

The Committee oversees the integrity of the Company's financial reporting, the effectiveness of its internal controls and risk management, the work of internal audit, and the relationship with the external auditor. It also advises the Board on whether the annual report, taken as a whole, is fair, balanced and understandable.

### Significant issues considered

The Committee focused on four areas during the year.

**Inventory valuation.** Inventories are a large balance and require judgement in assessing slow-moving stock and in the allocation of overheads. The Committee reviewed management's provisioning approach, challenged the assumptions, and discussed the work of the auditor, including physical count attendance at selected sites. It concluded that the balances were appropriately stated.

**Goodwill and impairment.** The Committee examined the impairment assessments, including the forecasts and discount rates used for the cash-generating units. It spent considerable time on the unit that was ultimately impaired and was satisfied that the amounts recorded were reasonable and the disclosures adequate.

**Revenue recognition on service contracts.** The Committee reviewed the accounting for long-term maintenance contracts and the estimates of progress used for revenue.

**Restructuring and one-off items.** The Committee considered the presentation of non-recurring charges and the consistency of the adjusted measures used in external communications.

### External auditor

Marlowe & Finch LLP served as the Company's external auditor for fiscal year 2025. The Committee assessed the quality of the audit through a structured questionnaire, meetings with senior finance staff, and a review of the auditor's own quality indicators. It concluded that the audit had been robust and that the auditor had shown appropriate scepticism. Total fees paid to the auditor were $3.9 million, of which non-audit services accounted for less than 8%. The Committee has approved a policy that restricts the types of non-audit work the auditor may perform.

### Independence of the Chair

Before joining the Board, I was a partner at Marlowe & Finch LLP. In view of that background, the Committee considered carefully whether my appointment as its chair could affect the perceived independence of the audit relationship. The Committee confirmed that the five-year cooling-off period required under the Company's policy had elapsed since I left the firm. I have no continuing financial arrangements with the firm other than a pension entitlement that does not depend on its performance, and I take no part in the Committee's decisions on the auditor's remuneration or appointment. The Board has reviewed and accepted this position, which will be reassessed annually.

### Internal control and internal audit

Internal audit completed 22 reviews during the year, covering procurement, treasury, IT general controls and several operating sites. Most findings were rated low or medium, and management has plans to address those that were not. The Committee also received a summary of control lessons arising from the interruption at the Dunmarrow plant and asked internal audit to follow up on the implementation of contingency planning improvements.

### Whistleblowing

The Committee reviewed reports received through the Company's confidential reporting line. There were 14 reports during the year, all of which were investigated. None was found to involve a material misstatement of the financial statements.

### Conclusion

The Committee is satisfied that the financial statements are properly prepared and that the Company's controls remain effective. It has recommended that the Board approve the annual report and that the auditor be proposed for reappointment at the annual general meeting.
