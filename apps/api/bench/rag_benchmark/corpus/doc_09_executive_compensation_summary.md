# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Compensation Committee Report: Summary of Executive Pay Outcomes, Fiscal Year 2025

### Purpose and philosophy

The Compensation Committee sets pay for the executive team in a way that rewards sustained performance, discourages excessive risk-taking and keeps interests aligned with those of long-term shareholders. A significant part of every executive's package is variable, and most of that variable pay is paid in shares that vest over several years. The Committee reviews external benchmarks annually and uses a peer group of industrial companies of similar size and complexity.

### Components of pay

Executive pay has four main elements: base salary, an annual bonus, a long-term incentive plan made up of performance share units, and pensions and benefits. Base salaries for executive directors were increased by 3.0% in January 2025, in line with the general increase awarded to salaried employees in the same markets. There were no changes to pension arrangements. No special or one-off awards were made to any executive during the year.

### The annual bonus

The annual bonus for fiscal 2025 was measured against four financial and operational metrics, with the following weightings: free cash flow (40%), adjusted operating income (30%), return on invested capital (20%), and a combined measure of safety and environmental performance (10%). The Committee chose to put the greatest weight on cash because of its importance to the Company's funding strategy and because it is harder to flatter through accounting judgement than profit measures.

Free cash flow exceeded the plan target by a wide margin, supported by tight management of inventories and capital spending. Adjusted operating income finished close to target after the production interruption at the Dunmarrow plant, and return on invested capital came in a little below target. Safety performance exceeded the maximum level, aided by the best injury rate in the Company's history.

The Committee did not exercise discretion to adjust the formulaic outcome upwards or downwards. After considering the outcome in the round, it concluded that the result was a fair reflection of the year. As a result, Chief Executive Officer Ingrid Vasterholm received an annual bonus paid at 142% of target, within the cap on awards. The Chief Financial Officer's bonus was paid at 131% of target. Neither executive's outcome reflects any reduction for the restructuring or impairment charges, which were treated consistently with the way the bonus plan defines adjusted operating income.

### Long-term incentives

Performance share units granted in fiscal 2023 were assessed against three-year targets for relative total shareholder return and cumulative earnings per share. They vested at 87% of the maximum opportunity. Awards made in fiscal 2025 are subject to the same metrics, with a new return-on-capital underpin. Executives are required to hold shares equal to a multiple of their base salary, and shares that vest are subject to a two-year holding period after vesting, even after the executive has left.

### Clawback and malus

The Company maintains a clawback policy that applies to bonus and long-term incentive awards in the event of a material misstatement, misconduct or serious reputational damage. The Committee reviewed the policy this year and confirmed it covers all executive officers. No clawback was triggered in fiscal 2025.

### Pay ratio and engagement

The Committee considers the ratio of CEO pay to median employee pay when it sets policy, but does not treat it as a target. The Chair of the Committee spoke with shareholders representing a substantial proportion of the share register in the autumn and received broadly supportive feedback, with questions mainly about the weighting of the cash metric and the inclusion of the return-on-capital underpin.
