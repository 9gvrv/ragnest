# Internal Memorandum: Capital Expenditure Plan, Fiscal Year 2026

**To:** Executive Leadership Team
**From:** Group Financial Planning
**Subject:** Summary of the capital plan presented to the Board

### Context

This memo summarises the capital plan for fiscal 2026 and the reasoning behind its main allocations. It supersedes earlier drafts. Detailed project papers are held by Financial Planning and are available on request.

The plan reflects three priorities set by the executive team: replacing capacity lost through the closure of an older facility, strengthening the resilience of our largest plants, and continuing to invest in automation and digital tools where payback is short. It also assumes that no acquisition spending will be funded from this envelope, which is treated separately.

### Total envelope

The envelope for the year comes to $418 million, which is slightly above the level of the year just ended and reflects the need to rebuild capacity at the same time as maintaining the regular programme. Roughly a quarter of the total is classified as maintenance capital, with the balance categorised as growth or efficiency investment. The Board has delegated approval of individual projects above a stated threshold to the Investment Committee, which will meet quarterly to review progress against budgets.

### Principal allocations

| Project area | Allocation |
|---|---|
| New Kessendal plant | $142 million |
| Dunmarrow coolant system rebuild and contingency capacity | $96 million |
| Fluid Controls automation and test equipment | $88 million |
| Industrial Services fleet and diagnostic equipment | $54 million |
| Digital and IT | $38 million |

### Commentary on the largest items

The Kessendal plant is the single largest commitment in the plan. It will take over the casting and finishing work that was previously done at the Halbrook foundry, which has now closed. A site has been secured and early groundworks begin in the first quarter. The new facility will use more automated pouring and handling equipment, and it will have lower energy intensity than the plant it replaces. Spending will be phased over this and the following year, and most of the first-year budget is for buildings and long-lead machinery.

The rebuild of Dunmarrow's cooling system follows the interruption described in the results announcement. Beyond the replacement of failed equipment, the project adds redundancy so that a single fault cannot take the whole plant offline, and it provides for a degree of spare machining capacity at other Precision Components sites.

### Risks to the plan

The main risks are construction cost inflation, delivery lead times for large machine tools, and permitting at Kessendal. Financial Planning has included a contingency within each project, but not at the portfolio level. If costs rise materially, the Investment Committee will be asked to decide whether to defer lower-priority items in the Digital and IT line or in fleet replacement.

### Next steps

Project leads should submit their quarterly spending forecasts to Financial Planning by the first week of each quarter. Questions on the plan should be directed to the Financial Planning mailbox.
