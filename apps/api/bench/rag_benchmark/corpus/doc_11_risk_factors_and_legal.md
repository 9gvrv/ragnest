# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Principal Risks and Legal Proceedings, Fiscal Year 2025

### How we manage risk

The Board is responsible for the Company's overall approach to risk. The Audit Committee oversees the effectiveness of the risk management and internal control systems, and each executive owns a defined set of risks. The principal risks below are not listed in order of importance, and the list is not exhaustive. Other risks that the Company does not currently consider material may later become so.

### Operational risks

**Plant availability.** A significant part of the Company's output is concentrated in a small number of large production sites. A fire, equipment failure, natural hazard or labour dispute at one of them could interrupt supply to customers, increase costs and damage relationships. The Company holds business interruption insurance, but coverage may not be sufficient for every loss and does not compensate for reputational damage. The events of the past year have reinforced management's view that contingency planning must be improved.

**Raw materials and energy.** Prices for specialty steels, tungsten carbide and other inputs fluctuate significantly. The Company passes through some cost increases to customers, but with a delay and not in every contract. Energy prices in Europe remain a concern for energy-intensive casting and heat-treatment operations.

**Supplier concentration.** For certain sealing materials and electronic components, the Company relies on a small number of qualified suppliers. Qualification of alternative sources can take a year or more.

### Financial risks

**Interest rates and refinancing.** The Company has reduced its exposure to floating rates but remains exposed to the cost of refinancing future maturities. **Currency.** Roughly half of revenue is earned outside the United States, and movements in the euro, the Polish zloty and several Gulf currencies affect reported results. **Credit.** Customers are mostly large utilities and industrial groups, and credit losses have historically been low.

### Regulatory and compliance risks

The Company is subject to export controls, anti-bribery laws, environmental rules and product-safety regulations in every country where it operates. A breach could result in fines, loss of licences and exclusion from tenders. A mandatory compliance training programme was completed by more than 98% of relevant employees this year.

### Cyber and technology risks

Industrial control systems at our plants and the networks that link them are attractive targets for criminal groups. Although no incident in the year caused material loss, the Company continues to invest in monitoring, segmentation and staff awareness.

### Legal proceedings

The Company is party to a number of claims in the ordinary course of business, including product liability, employment and commercial disputes. Except as described below, management does not expect any of these to have a material adverse effect on the Company's financial position.

**Hallwick v. Tarnwick.** In the second quarter, a putative shareholder class action, Hallwick v. Tarnwick, was filed against the Company and two former officers in the Meridian District Court. The complaint alleges that the Company misstated the value of its inventories in certain earlier periods and that share prices were inflated as a result. The Company denies the allegations and intends to defend the case vigorously. A motion to dismiss is expected to be filed in due course. Having regard to the stage of the proceedings and the advice of counsel, the Company has recorded a reserve of $17.5 million in respect of the matter. The Company cannot predict the outcome, and the amount ultimately payable could be higher or lower than the reserve. The Audit Committee has reviewed the Company's inventory accounting and controls in light of the allegations, and the external auditor has considered the matter in its audit.

**Environmental matters.** The Company is involved in a small number of remediation exercises at legacy sites in the United States and Germany. Provisions are held where an obligation is probable and can be estimated, and none is individually material.

**Tax disputes.** Two transfer-pricing audits, in Poland and in a Gulf jurisdiction, are ongoing. Management believes that adequate provisions have been made and that the positions taken are supportable.

### Emerging risks

The Board also monitors risks that are less certain but potentially significant, including supply chain regulation, shifts in the water-infrastructure funding environment, and technology change in sensing and monitoring. These items are reviewed twice a year and are reported to the Board with management's proposed responses.
