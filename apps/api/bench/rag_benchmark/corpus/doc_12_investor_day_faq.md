# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Investor Questions and Answers, November 2025

*Selected questions from shareholders and analysts following the fiscal 2025 results. Answers are provided by the Investor Relations team and reviewed by the Chief Financial Officer.*

### Dividends

**How does the Board think about the dividend?**
The Board aims to pay a dividend that grows steadily over the cycle and is well covered by free cash flow, rather than tracking quarterly earnings. It also looks at leverage and investment needs before it decides on each payment. The interim payment in June was made in line with that approach.

**What is the final dividend for the year, and when will it be paid?**
The Board has declared a final dividend of $0.37 per share. It will be paid on 18 December 2025 to shareholders on the register at the close of business on 28 November 2025. Shareholders who participate in the dividend reinvestment plan should check the election deadline with the plan administrator.

**Will the dividend be affected by the proposed Corvane acquisition?**
No. As stated at the time of the announcement, the transaction does not change the Company's dividend policy.

### Share repurchases

**Does Tarnwick still plan to buy back shares?**
Yes, as a tool for returning surplus cash, but not as a fixed commitment. The Board has authorised repurchases of up to $225 million under a programme that runs until 31 March 2027. Purchases are made in the market, depend on the share price and liquidity at the time, and may be paused or stopped. Repurchased shares are cancelled.

**Why not use the authorisation more quickly?**
Management prefers to retain flexibility while the Company completes a significant acquisition and rebuilds capacity. Surplus cash will be reviewed again when the transaction has closed.

### Balance sheet

**What is the Company's leverage policy?**
Management aims to run the business at a net leverage ratio comfortably below the covenant in its credit facility, with room to invest through the cycle. It sees leverage in the range of two to two-and-a-half times as appropriate in normal conditions, and would accept a temporary increase for a strategic transaction provided there is a credible path back.

**Are there any near-term maturities?**
No scheduled maturities in the next two fiscal years. The Company has regular conversations with its lenders and with the rating agencies.

### Operations and outlook

**Is the Dunmarrow plant back to normal?**
Production has returned to normal rates, and the Company has commissioned an independent review to confirm the root cause. The planned investment in the plant's systems is part of next year's capital plan.

**What will drive margin recovery in fiscal 2026?**
Management expects three main contributors: the recovery in output at the affected plant, price realisation in Fluid Controls, and continued growth in higher-margin recurring service revenue. Raw material costs are not currently a significant headwind.

**Does the Company provide quarterly guidance?**
No. The Company gives an annual outlook and updates it when circumstances change significantly.

### Governance

**Where can I find the annual report and the notice of meeting?**
Both are published on the Investor Relations website. Printed copies can be requested from the Company Secretary's office. Shareholders can vote online, by post or in person at the annual general meeting, and are encouraged to submit questions in advance.
