# Tarnwick Industrial Group
## Fiscal Year 2024 Results: Highlights

*Published November 2024. This summary relates to the fiscal year ended 30 September 2024 and has been superseded by later publications.*

### Headline results

Sales for fiscal 2024 were $4,118.3 million. Net income attributable to shareholders was $291.0 million. The Company reported growth in each of its three operating segments, with Fluid Controls again the strongest performer. Management described the year as one of solid execution against a mixed economic background, with better pricing and improved services utilisation offsetting weaker volumes in some general-industrial markets.

### Dividend

The Board recommended a final dividend of $0.34 per share, to be paid on 19 December 2024. It noted that the total dividend for the year was higher than in the previous year and that cash coverage remained comfortable.

### People

The Company ended the year with 11,208 full-time employees, up modestly from the prior year as hiring in engineering and services offset attrition elsewhere. The lost-time injury rate improved for the third consecutive year.

### Capital allocation

The Board approved a capital expenditure budget of $402 million for fiscal 2025, focused on automation, tooling and the maintenance of the group's largest sites. It also authorised a share repurchase programme of up to $150 million, which was due to expire on 31 March 2026. Management said that the programme would be used opportunistically and that capital would be allocated first to organic investment.

### Financing

The Company's revolving credit facility, then in the amount of $600 million, was undrawn at the year end. The Company said it planned to review its financing arrangements during the coming year with the aim of extending the average maturity of its debt.

### Outlook at the time

Management expected low-single-digit growth in sales in fiscal 2025, with margins broadly stable and a continued focus on cash generation. It cautioned that customer destocking in parts of the portfolio might continue into the first half of the year.
